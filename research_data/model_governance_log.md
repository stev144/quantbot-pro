Append one entry per pair evaluated. Do not delete rejected entries — they

## AVAX_USDT / ATOM_USDT — REJECTED

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Verdict:** REJECTED — no statistically demonstrated edge. Do not deploy live.

### Reason 1: Permutation test fails on every metric

`research_data/permutation_test/AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT/permutation_verdict.csv`
(100 shuffles vs. the real run):

| metric | real value | percentile vs. shuffles | p-value | significant? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| win_rate | 0.9311 | 0.0 | 1.0 | False |
| sharpe_ratio | 19.44 | 0.01 | 0.99 | False |
| profit_factor | 12.96 | 0.0 | 1.0 | False |

`edge_appears_real = False`, `real_entry_ic_meaningfully_positive = False`.

The real (unshuffled) run doesn't just fail to beat the shuffles — it ranks at
or near the *bottom* of all 100 randomized permutations on every metric
(percentile ≈ 0). Shuffled versions of the same entry/exit rules routinely
produce *higher* win rates (0.96–0.98 vs. real 0.9311) and *higher* Sharpe
(17–45 vs. real 19.44). If randomizing the signal improves the outcome, the
outcome isn't coming from the signal.

### Reason 2: entry_ic is near-zero-to-negative in every fold, not just noisy

Full-period backtest (`AVAX_ATOM_strategy_summary.csv`): `entry_ic = -0.0791`.

Walk-forward (`walk_forward/AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT/AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT_walk_forward_folds.csv`),
all 3 folds computed fresh (`used_fallback_params = False`, no fallback constants involved):

| fold | train_entry_ic | test_entry_ic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | -0.0372 | -0.0887 |
| 2 | -0.0592 | -0.2107 |
| 3 | -0.0983 | +0.0216 |

Every train IC is negative; 2 of 3 test ICs are negative and the third
(+0.0216) is indistinguishable from noise. There is no fold in which the
entry signal shows real forward-looking skill. This matches the permutation
shuffles, which themselves center around entry_ic ≈ -0.02 to -0.09 — i.e.
the "real" signal's IC sits inside the noise distribution generated by
randomizing it, not outside it.

### Reason 3: the 93% win rate is a mechanical artifact of the exit rule, not skill

Traced via the trade log (`AVAX_ATOM_trade_log.csv`, 842 trades) and the raw
`kalman_zscore` series (`AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT_kalman.csv`, 43,311 candles):

- Win/loss size is roughly symmetric (avg winner +0.97%, avg loser -1.02%,
  ratio 0.96) — the high win rate is a **frequency** effect, not a
  risk/reward-skew effect.
- `EXIT_ZSCORE_TARGET = 0.3` sits inside the zone the z-score occupies
  **30.5%** of all unconditional time (it's just the neighborhood of the
  mean of a mean-zero, ~unit-std series).
- `EXIT_ZSCORE_STOPLOSS = 4.5` sits inside a zone the z-score occupies only
  **0.36%** of the time — genuinely rare, ~85x rarer than the target zone.
- `kalman_zscore` is winsorised at `ZSCORE_WINSOR_LIMIT = ±5.0`
  (`kalman_filter_engine.py`), i.e. structurally clipped, and the observed
  max across the full dataset is exactly 5.0. The stop sits only 0.5 below
  a hard ceiling the metric can never exceed, which further suppresses stop
  hits independent of any directional edge.
- A same-distance symmetric-stop simulation against each trade's
  `max_adverse_zscore` only flips ~1.2% of winning trades to losers
  (92.0% vs. actual 93.1%) — the asymmetry in threshold *distance* is not
  the driver either. The win rate is explained almost entirely by "target
  = common reversion toward a self-normalized mean" vs. "stop = rare,
  possibly-clipped tail event," which requires no forecasting skill at all.

### Stale constants — do not reuse as defaults for other pairs

`bot/research/entry_exit_engine.py:172-174` hardcodes:

```python
VALIDATED_IC:        float = 0.5245
VALIDATED_WIN_RATE:  float = 0.6900
VALIDATED_HALF_LIFE: float = 119.9
```

`VALIDATED_HALF_LIFE` is accurate (matches `cointegration_pairs.csv` and all
walk-forward folds at ~119.9-120h). **`VALIDATED_IC` and `VALIDATED_WIN_RATE`
are stale/invalid** — they contradict every fresh computation cited above
(actual IC clusters near zero-to-negative, not 0.5245; actual win rate is
~93%, not 69%) and are also used as fallback defaults inside
`walk_forward_engine.py` when a fold's own TRAIN slice can't produce an
estimate. Given AVAX/ATOM itself is rejected, these two constants should not
be carried forward as assumed-good defaults when evaluating any other pair —
doing so would seed a new pair's fallback path with numbers that were never
actually validated in the first place.

## DOT_USDT / LINK_USDT — REJECTED

**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Verdict:** REJECTED — no statistically demonstrated edge, same failure
pattern as AVAX/ATOM. Do not deploy live.

This pair never should have reached entry/exit simulation in the first
place — `cointegration_engine.py` itself rejected it — but it was
deliberately tested anyway (via `entry_exit_engine.py --allow-filtered-pair`,
added specifically for this) to check whether the pipeline's verdicts are
*consistent*, not just to see if this one pair happened to work.

### Reason 1: failed cointegration's own half-life filter — not a borderline miss

`research_data/cointegration_pairs.csv`: half-life = **196.84h** vs. the
120h ceiling — 64% over, not a boundary-effect case. `is_cointegrated=True`
(the ADF/coint p-values pass), but a spread this slow to revert means any
position is held far longer, with far more exposure to the cointegrating
relationship itself breaking down before reversion completes.

### Reason 2: failed walk-forward, narrowly, but on the metric that doesn't loosen

`research_data/walk_forward/walk_forward_leaderboard.csv`: OOS win rate
90.6%, Sharpe 14.9, profit factor 7.7, WFE 0.89 — all comfortably clear
their thresholds. **Failed only on max drawdown: -20.27% vs. the -20.00%
cutoff** (`OOS_MAX_DRAWDOWN_PCT`, which explicitly does not loosen
out-of-sample, unlike the other three thresholds). Genuinely borderline,
unlike the half-life miss.

### Reason 3: permutation test fails on every metric — same pattern as AVAX/ATOM

To test this pair honestly despite its rejected half-life, two gaps in the
pipeline had to be closed first (see "Fixes made to test this pair" below).
With those in place, 100 block-shuffled replicas vs. the real run
(`research_data/permutation_test/DOT_USDT_LINK_USDT/permutation_verdict.csv`):

| metric | real value | percentile vs. shuffles | p-value | significant? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| win_rate | 0.905 | 0.0 | 1.0 | False |
| sharpe_ratio | 15.93 | 0.0 | 1.0 | False |
| profit_factor | 8.93 | 0.0 | 1.0 | False |

`edge_appears_real = False`, `real_entry_ic = -0.0899`,
`real_entry_ic_meaningfully_positive = False`.

Identical shape to AVAX/ATOM: the real (unshuffled) result ranks at the
*bottom* of all 100 randomized permutations on every metric, and entry_ic is
negative. Randomizing the data produces better results than the real
strategy — same conclusion, same magnitude, same sign on entry_ic (-0.0899
vs. AVAX/ATOM's -0.0791). The suspiciously high win rate here is very likely
the same mechanical target/stop-zone artifact documented in AVAX/ATOM's
Reason 3 above, since both pairs run through the identical

DOT/LINK, but there is no reason to expect the mechanism to behave
differently for a different pair's z-score series.

### Fixes made to test this pair (now general, not DOT/LINK-specific)

Testing a cointegration-rejected pair honestly required closing two gaps
where the pipeline silently substituted AVAX/ATOM's numbers for any pair
it couldn't look up:

1. `run_entry_exit_simulation()` (`entry_exit_engine.py`) now accepts
   `require_passes_filters` (CLI: `--allow-filtered-pair`), threading it
   into `load_pair_config()` instead of always requiring
   `passes_filters=True`. Without it, DOT/LINK's half-life lookup would
   raise and silently fall back to AVAX/ATOM's 119.9h.
2. `exit_time_stop_hours` is now derived as `2 * validated_half_life` per
   pair, instead of always using the module's `EXIT_TIME_STOP_HOURS=240`
   default (which only ever matched AVAX/ATOM's own half-life by
   coincidence: 2×119.9≈240). DOT/LINK's correct time-stop is 394h.
3. `permutation_test_engine.py`'s `_run_engine_on_df()` constructed
   `EntryExitEngine` directly, bypassing both fixes above entirely (its
   scratch filename, `shuffled_kalman.csv`, doesn't match the
   `<SYMBOL_A>_<SYMBOL_B>_kalman.csv` convention `EntryExitEngine.run()`
   parses identity from) — every permutation-test run, real or shuffled,
   for any pair, was silently using AVAX/ATOM's identity and parameters
   regardless of which pair was actually being tested. `run()` now resolves
   pair identity and half-life once and threads them into every engine it
   builds.

For this specific run, fix #2 turned out not to change the numbers — no
DOT/LINK trade held long enough to hit either the old 240h or the correct
394h time-stop — but the fix stands for future pairs where it will matter,
and fix #3 was necessary for this permutation test to reflect DOT/LINK at
all rather than silently re-testing AVAX/ATOM under a different label.

### Strategic implication

Two pairs tested end-to-end through this pipeline; both rejected by the
permutation test, both via the same failure signature (bottom-percentile
real result, negative entry_ic). The pipeline is behaving *consistently* —
it correctly flags both as lacking real edge — but that consistency itself
is the finding: the current `entry_exit_engine.py` rule set (z-score
entry/exit thresholds, target/stop asymmetry) has not yet demonstrated edge
on any pair tested, not just an unlucky one. Before testing further pairs
with the same fixed rule set, consider whether the entry/exit rules
themselves need to change, rather than searching for a pair they happen to
work on.

## Structural bias audit — fixed-absolute stop level and STRONG position sizing

**Date:** 2026-08-03

Investigated *why* both pairs above show negative entry_ic, rather than just
that they do. Found a concrete, cross-pair-consistent mechanical bias
unrelated to either pair's actual economics, fixed it, and reran both
permutation tests to confirm what changed and what didn't.

### The bias

`EXIT_ZSCORE_STOPLOSS = 4.5` was a **fixed absolute** z-level checked against
every trade regardless of its own entry point. Since entries range from 2.0
to 4.5+, a trade entered at |z|=2.1 had ~2.4 z-units of room before its stop;
one entered at |z|=3.8 had only ~0.7. Bucketing real trades by entry |z|
showed stop-rate rising with entry magnitude almost identically on both
unrelated pairs:

| Entry \|z\| bucket | AVAX/ATOM stop-rate | DOT/LINK stop-rate |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0–2.3 | 2.6% | 4.1% |
| 2.6–3.0 | 9.4% | 10.8% |
| 3.5+ | 32.5% | 33.6% |

`IC(|entry_z| -> is_winner) = -0.20` on both pairs (p<0.0001 both). Decomposed:
within winners only, IC ≈ 0 (not significant); within losers only, IC ≈
+0.80 (highly significant) — the entire negative entry_ic came from
"bigger entries get stopped out more often," a pure geometry effect, not
"bigger entries produce smaller wins."

This compounded with `KalmanPositionSizer`'s STRONG-signal multiplier
(1.5x size for |z| >= 3.0), justified in its own docstring as "higher
z-scores have higher win rates." The real data said the opposite on both
pairs: STRONG win rate 83.5% (AVAX/ATOM) / 79.8% (DOT/LINK) vs. NORMAL
95.5% / 93.8%. The strategy was sizing up its worst-performing bucket.

### Fixes applied (`bot/research/entry_exit_engine.py`)

1. Added `EXIT_ZSCORE_STOP_DISTANCE = 1.5` and `TradeRecord.stop_zscore`
   (computed once at entry as `min(|entry_z| + 1.5, ZSCORE_WINSOR_LIMIT)`).
   `_check_exit_conditions()` now checks each trade against its own
   `stop_zscore` instead of the shared `EXIT_ZSCORE_STOPLOSS`. 1.5 (not the
   old threshold-entry distance of 2.5) was chosen deliberately smaller so
   the capped stop stays reachable — and therefore actually protective —
   for the large majority of entries instead of silently disabling stop
   protection for the most extreme ones.
2. `KalmanPositionSizer.size_position()`'s signal multiplier is now fixed at
   1.0 for both STRONG and NORMAL — `signal_strength` is kept as a label for
   analysis but no longer changes position size.

### Result of rerunning both permutation tests post-fix

| metric | AVAX/ATOM before → after | DOT/LINK before → after |
|---|---|---|
| entry_ic | -0.0791 (p=0.02, sig.) → **+0.0327 (p=0.34, n.s.)** | -0.0899 (p=0.006, sig.) → **+0.0475 (p=0.15, n.s.)** |
| win_rate percentile vs. shuffles | 0.0 → 0.0 | 0.0 → 0.0 |
| sharpe percentile vs. shuffles | 0.01 → 0.03 | 0.0 → 0.0 |
| `edge_appears_real` | False → **False** | False → **False** |

The fix worked exactly as intended: the negative entry_ic bias is gone on
both pairs, replaced by a small, non-significant value centered near zero —
i.e. no fabricated edge was introduced, the artifact was simply removed.

**Verdict for both pairs is unchanged: still REJECTED.** This is expected,
not a failure of the fix. What was fixed is distinct from Reason 3 above
(target zone = common region any bounded/mean-reverting z-score visits ~30%
of the time; stop zone = rare region near the winsor clip). A trade-relative
stop still sits in a rare tail of the z-score's own distribution — it just
relocates *which* rare level each trade uses — so the permutation test
correctly continues to flag the deeper target/stop-zone asymmetry as the
dominant driver of the win rate, on both pairs, independent of the
now-fixed entry-magnitude bias.

## Why entry_ic won't move without further work — target/stop zone geometry and an unexplained return-scaling gap

**Date:** 2026-08-03

Follow-up investigation into *why* entry_ic sits at a small, non-significant
value post-fix (+0.0327 / +0.0475 above) rather than significantly positive.
Ad-hoc analysis against the post-fix real-run trade logs
(`research_data/permutation_test/AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT/real_run/AVAX_ATOM_trade_log.csv`,
`research_data/permutation_test/DOT_USDT_LINK_USDT/real_run/DOT_LINK_trade_log.csv`)
and the full `kalman_zscore`/`kalman_spread` series
(`research_data/AVAX_USDT_ATOM_USDT_kalman.csv`,
`research_data/DOT_USDT_LINK_USDT_kalman.csv`); no new engine code was run,
this is analysis of existing outputs. No script from this pass was committed
to the repo.

### Target/stop zone occupancy is symmetric across pairs, confirming it's a rule-design property, not a pair-specific effect

Unconditional occupancy of the current thresholds (`EXIT_ZSCORE_TARGET =
0.3`, trade-relative stop = `min(|entry_z| + EXIT_ZSCORE_STOP_DISTANCE, ZSCORE_WINSOR_LIMIT)`):

| | AVAX/ATOM | DOT/LINK |
|---|---|---|
| Target zone (`\|z\|≤0.3`) occupancy | 30.50% | 31.35% |
| Overall avg stop level (post-fix) | 4.056 | 4.081 |
| Occupancy of that stop level (`\|z\|≥stop`) | 0.563% | 0.600% |
| Ratio (target rarity vs. stop rarity) | 54.1x | 52.2x |

Per entry bucket, stop-zone occupancy stays in the 0.23%–0.87% range on both
pairs even for the smallest, least-extreme entries — nowhere near the ~30%
target occupancy. The two unrelated pairs land on nearly identical numbers,
confirming this asymmetry is inherent to any target-near-mean/stop-in-tail
mean-reversion design, **not a bug**: it's why win_rate/Sharpe/profit_factor
look strong regardless of entry skill (shuffled permutations preserve the
same zone geometry and match or beat the real run on those metrics — see
Reason 3/permutation results above). `entry_ic` is the metric that actually
isolates entry-timing skill from this geometry, which is why it — not
win_rate/Sharpe — is the one to move.

### entry_ic is flat even restricted to trades that fully complete reversion (rules out stop/time-stop dilution)

Splitting trades by exit reason (TARGET = 92.6–92.8% of trades on both
pairs, STOPLOSS = 7.2–7.5%, no TIMESTOP exits occurred in either full-period
run):

| | AVAX/ATOM | DOT/LINK |
|---|---|---|
| Overall entry_ic | +0.0327 (p=0.34) | +0.0475 (p=0.15) |
| TARGET-exit only | +0.0256 (p=0.48) | +0.0385 (p=0.26) |
| STOPLOSS-exit only | +0.1712 (p=0.19) | +0.3700 (p=0.0018) |

Restricting to TARGET-exit trades — the 93% of trades that aren't cut short
by a stop — doesn't recover a positive IC. So the flat overall entry_ic
isn't an artifact of stop/time-stop trades diluting an otherwise-real
relationship; it's flat even in the trades that ran to full completion.
(The positive STOPLOSS-only IC is a separate, purely mechanical effect: the
trade-relative stop distance is fixed at `entry_z + 1.5`, so a bigger entry
that does get stopped out necessarily loses more — this is geometry, not
skill, and doesn't bear on the TARGET-exit result.)

### Two independent mechanisms both push realized P&L toward flat regardless of entry depth

Entry z-score reliably predicts the *z-space* distance a trade travels
(`IC(entry_z, z_distance_traveled) = +0.97` both pairs — near-mechanical,
since the target is fixed at 0.3). If P&L scaled with that, entry_ic would
be strongly positive. It doesn't, for two separate, independently-confirmed
reasons:

**Mechanism 1 — the z-score compresses real distance at the tail.**
Comparing z-space distance to the *raw* `kalman_spread` distance actually
traveled (joined from the kalman CSVs by exit timestamp):

| entry bucket | z_distance ratio (vs. smallest bucket) | raw spread distance ratio (vs. smallest bucket) |
|---|---|---|
| 2.0–2.3 | 1.00x | 1.00x |
| 2.6–3.0 | ~1.3x | 1.28–1.38x |
| 3.5+ | ~2.05x | **1.56–1.89x** |

`IC(entry_z, raw_spread_distance) = +0.36 to +0.50` — much weaker than the
+0.97 for z-space distance. A z=4 entry is not actually ~2x further from
equilibrium in real spread terms the way the z-metric implies; part of what
makes deep entries "look" extreme is `kalman_filter_engine.py`'s
normalizer (the uncertainty/volatility term in the z-score denominator),
not pure economics.

**Mechanism 2 — even the real raw distance traveled doesn't convert
proportionally into % return, for reasons not yet identified.**
`IC(entry_z, net_pnl_pct / raw_distance) = -0.36 to -0.50` (p < 1e-25 both
pairs) — i.e. even correcting for mechanism 1, trades that travel further in
genuine raw-spread terms still don't earn proportionally more. Three
candidate explanations were tested and **ruled out**:

- *Kalman estimation noise at entry* (`prediction_error_at_entry`): despite
  correlating with entry_z on its own (IC +0.36/+0.50), a median split into
  low-/high-noise halves shows the efficiency decline unchanged in both
  halves (IC -0.82/-0.92 low vs. -0.77/-0.94 high), and the rank-partial
  correlation controlling for it (-0.87/-0.85) barely differs from the
  unconditional -0.88/-0.89. Not the cause.
- *Hedge-ratio (`entry_beta`) instability*: `entry_beta` essentially doesn't
  correlate with entry_z (IC +0.004 p=0.91 AVAX/ATOM, +0.032 p=0.35
  DOT/LINK), and controlling for `|entry_beta - median_beta|` doesn't move
  the entry_z→efficiency relationship (partial IC -0.364/-0.496 vs.
  unconditional -0.364/-0.499). Not the cause.
- *Price-level/era clustering*: deep-entry trades aren't concentrated in any
  particular period — `IC(entry_z, entry_timestamp) ≈ 0` (p=0.64/0.09) and
  every entry-depth bucket spans the full 2020–2025 backtest window roughly
  evenly. Not the cause.

### Strategic implication

Target/stop zone asymmetry (54x/52x) explains why win_rate/Sharpe/profit_factor
are structurally poor discriminators of edge for this rule family — that's
expected, not fixable-nor-worth-fixing, since it's inherent to any
mean-reversion target/stop design. The metric that does isolate entry-timing
skill, entry_ic, stays flat for two confirmed, independent reasons: the
z-score's own normalization compresses real distance at extreme readings
(traceable to `kalman_filter_engine.py`), and a second, still-unexplained
effect that dilutes returns per unit of *real* distance traveled at deep
entries even after accounting for that compression. Three plausible causes
of the second effect (estimation noise, hedge-ratio drift, time-regime
clustering) have been ruled out. Before spending further effort chasing the
second mechanism's cause, or attempting a z-score renormalization fix for
the first, worth deciding whether this line of investigation (making the
current z-score-threshold entry rule itself skillful) is a better use of
effort than testing a structurally different entry signal.

## P&L normalisation bug found and fixed — both pairs re-tested, verdict partially reversed

**Date:** 2026-08-14

**What was found:** `entry_exit_engine.py`'s `_execute_full_exit()` and
`_execute_partial_exit()` computed `gross_pnl_pct = spread_move /
abs(entry_spread)` — normalising the trade's raw log-spread move by the
spread's own *level* at entry. `entry_spread` is a mean-reverting quantity
that sits near zero by construction (the whole premise of the strategy is
that it's several std-devs from its own rolling mean at entry, but its raw
level has no relationship to trade risk or size) — dividing by it is a
category error, analogous to computing a stock's return as
`price_change / distance-from-its-own-moving-average` instead of
`price_change / price`.

**Evidence this was actually wrong**, not just theoretically questionable:
the formula produced a mean `gross_pnl_pct` of **83–97% per trade** across
both AVAX/ATOM and DOT/LINK's real (unshuffled) trade logs — not
economically plausible for a market-neutral pairs strategy. Checking the
raw, undivided `spread_move` directly showed it already averages ~2.7% in
magnitude — exactly the range expected for this kind of trade. Since
`spread_t = log(AVAX_t) - α_t - β_t × log(ATOM_t)` is already a log-return-
equivalent linear combination of the two legs, the raw move *is* the
correct return; no additional normalisation was needed or justified.

**The fix:** `gross_pnl_pct = np.expm1(spread_move)` (expm1 converts the
log-return to a simple return for precision; identical to `spread_move`
itself to ~4 decimal places at these magnitudes — the division was the
entire bug, not a missing conversion).

**Why this was missed by every prior check in this log:** every downstream
metric (win_rate, Sharpe, profit_factor, entry_ic, the permutation test)
is internally consistent under either formula — nothing crashed or produced
an obviously-malformed number from a code-correctness standpoint. Only
comparing the resulting P&L *magnitude* against economic plausibility
(a market-neutral spread trade should not average 90% per trade) caught it.
This also means every number in every entry above this one in this log —
both pairs' win rates, Sharpes, entry_ic values, walk-forward folds, and
permutation-test verdicts — was computed on top of the broken formula.

### Interpretation — genuinely mixed, not a clean reversal

`entry_ic` — the metric specifically designed to isolate entry-timing skill
from the exit rule's own geometry — is now robustly, significantly
positive on **both** independently-tested pairs (0.35 and 0.44), a finding
that **replicates cross-pair**, the same bar this log has applied to every
other claim. That's real: the broken P&L formula was adding enough noise
to the numerator that it was drowning out a genuine relationship between
entry z-score magnitude and realised return.

`win_rate`/`sharpe_ratio` are **still** not statistically distinguishable
from 100 randomly shuffled versions of the same rules, on both pairs — this
is unrelated to the P&L formula and unchanged by fixing it. Per the earlier
"Why entry_ic won't move" investigation in this log, shuffled replicas
preserve the same target-zone/stop-zone occupancy asymmetry (~30% vs
~0.5%) that any entry signal — real or randomised — gets funnelled through
by this exit design, which is why win_rate/Sharpe stay structurally
uninformative about real skill for this rule family regardless of the
entry signal's quality.

Because `edge_appears_real` requires all three conditions
(`sharpe_significant AND win_rate_significant AND
real_entry_ic_meaningfully_positive`), the formal verdict is still
**REJECTED** by this pipeline's own bar. But the reason has changed
materially: it is no longer "the entry signal shows no skill" (the original
Aug-2/Aug-3 verdicts above) — it is now "the entry signal shows real,
replicated skill, but the current exit-rule design (fixed near-zero target
regardless of entry depth) doesn't convert that skill into an overall
result distinguishable from noise."

### Strategic implication

The entry signal (Kalman z-score magnitude) appears to carry genuine,
cross-pair-replicated predictive power that was previously invisible due to
a P&L calculation bug, not a signal-quality problem. The blocker is now
squarely the **exit rule design**, specifically its insensitivity to entry
depth: every trade targets the same near-zero z-level regardless of how
extreme its entry was, which is exactly the mechanism the "Why entry_ic
won't move" investigation traced the target/stop occupancy asymmetry to.
A natural next step — not yet attempted — is an exit/sizing design that
scales with entry depth (e.g. a profit target or position size proportional
to `|entry_z|`, rather than a fixed `EXIT_ZSCORE_TARGET = 0.3` for every
entry) so that a demonstrably-better entry (larger `|z|`, higher entry_ic-
implied edge) is actually harvested as proportionally more realised return,
instead of being capped at the same target as a marginal entry. This is
still REJECTED for live deployment as currently built — this is a lead for
further research, not a green light.

## First entry-depth-scaled exit design attempt — tested, did not fix the verdict

**Date:** 2026-08-14

Following the lead from the entry above, implemented and fully audited the
first candidate fix for the target/stop occupancy asymmetry.

### Design

`entry_exit_engine.py`: replaced the fixed `EXIT_ZSCORE_TARGET = 0.3`
(exit once `|z| <= 0.3`, same for every trade) with a per-trade,
entry-depth-scaled target requiring the spread to cross **through**
equilibrium to the *opposite* side, computed once at entry:

```
target_zscore = -sign(entry_zscore) * EXIT_TARGET_OVERSHOOT_FRACTION * |entry_zscore|
# EXIT_TARGET_OVERSHOOT_FRACTION = 0.10
```

Rationale: `|z| <= 0.3` is a wide neighbourhood any mean-reverting series
wanders into ~30% of unconditional time regardless of entry skill — that's
the property block-shuffling preserves, which is why win_rate/Sharpe kept
failing significance even after entry_ic was confirmed real. Requiring an
actual sign-crossing, scaled to entry depth, was a bet that "did the series
really complete reversion" is a more temporally/causally dependent (and
therefore shuffle-sensitive) event than "did it wander near its own mean."

### Result: the bet didn't pay off — full audit, both pairs

| Sharpe | 17.12 | **20.80** | 13.19 | **14.73** |
| Time-stop exits | 0 | **0** | — | — |

Win rate and Sharpe went **up**, not down, under the new design — the
opposite of the intended effect — and zero trades hit the 240h time-stop
even with the sign-crossing requirement in place. Both facts point to the
same explanation: this z-series crosses back through its own rolling mean
so frequently (median hold time 2-3h, vs. a 120h half-life) that requiring
a confirmed crossing was nearly as easy to satisfy as the old "approach
near zero" rule. The lever didn't raise the bar by much because the series'
own oscillation frequency is high relative to the bar being raised.

### What a next attempt should try differently

The 0.10 overshoot fraction and hourly candle resolution were likely too
gentle relative to how fast this z-series actually moves. Directions not
yet tried, roughly in order of how directly they target the diagnosed
mechanism:

1. **A much larger overshoot fraction** (e.g. 0.5-1.0, requiring the

   not just 10% of it) — the current 0.10 barely raised the bar at all
   given how often this series crosses zero; a fraction this small was
   always a weak test of the hypothesis, not a refutation of the general
   approach.
2. **A minimum holding period before TARGET can trigger** — since median
   hold time is only 2-3h, a same-candle or next-candle reversion is

3. **A lower candle resolution** (4h/daily instead of 1h) for entry/exit

   entry/exit rule may be dominated by microstructure noise the original
   half-life (120h) estimate never assumed the rules would react to
   candle-by-candle.

None of these are validated — each needs the same full audit (entry/exit
sim -> walk-forward -> permutation test, both pairs) this entry and the one
before it went through. Still REJECTED for live deployment.

## Third design attempt — lower candle resolution (4h) — also rejected, marginal movement in the same noise-level range as the prior two attempts

**Date:** 2026-08-15

Direction #3 from the prior entry's list, the one remaining untried lever

### Design

`entry_exit_engine.py`: added a `resample_hours` parameter (new module
constant `CANDLE_RESAMPLE_HOURS`, default `None` = off) that resamples the
already-validated 1h `kalman_zscore` series to a lower frequency — taking
the last 1h reading in each N-hour window — **before** scanning for
entries/exits at all, so a decision only sees a fresh z-score reading
every N hours instead of every 1h tick. `kalman_zscore_lag1` is recomputed
from the resampled series (the original column was a 1h lag, meaningless
once each row spans N hours). Tested at `resample_hours=4`, on top of the

`walk_forward_engine.py` required two correctness fixes, both because
those modules had hardcoded assumptions that every candle is 1h wide:

1. `permutation_test_engine.py`'s adaptive block-size calculation

   clipping to `[MIN_BLOCK_SIZE_CANDLES, MAX_BLOCK_SIZE_CANDLES]`.
2. `walk_forward_engine.py` had `CANDLE_HOURS = 1.0` hardcoded into its

   candle-count fold-validity floor. Both silently assume 1h candles;
   left unscaled, a 4h resample would have recalibrated half-life 4x too

## Fourth design attempt — entry-depth-scaled minimum hold — still rejected, but the first attempt to produce a real, same-direction movement on both pairs

EXIT_MIN_HOLD_BASE_HOURS: float = 24.0   # min hold at |z| = ENTRY_ZSCORE_THRESHOLD (2.0)

obviously the right next move for both pairs simultaneously — it may only

## Foundational correction — every design attempt to date has calibrated time constants off the WRONG half-life

**Date:** 2026-09-14

Stepped back from tuning exit-rule parameters entirely and ran a real
statistical characterization (descriptive stats, Jarque-Bera normality,
dual ADF+KPSS stationarity, ACF/Ljung-Box autocorrelation, a formal AR(1)
half-life re-estimation, and leg-return covariance/correlation) on all
three pairs' actual traded series — not another strategy backtest.

### The finding

`validated_half_life` (77h / 120h / 197h, from `cointegration_engine.py`)
is estimated on the STATIC OLS spread's TRAINING WINDOW only. Refitting
the identical AR(1)/OU regression on the SAME static (`ols_spread`) column
but over the FULL post-warmup dataset gives R^2 = 0.0001-0.0012 (thousands
of hours implied half-life, i.e. no detectable mean reversion at all) —
confirming, via a different method, what the OOS-persistence audit already
found: the static relationship doesn't generalize past its training window.

But `entry_exit_engine.py` does not trade the static spread — every entry/
exit decision uses `kalman_zscore`/`kalman_spread` (the adaptive-hedge-
ratio series). Refitting the SAME AR(1) regression on `kalman_spread`
gives a REAL, robust signal across the FULL dataset (R^2 = 0.13-0.15, not
spurious) with an implied half-life of **2.3-2.7 hours** on all three
pairs — not 30-80x that.

| pair | documented half-life (static OLS, training window) | kalman_spread AR(1) half-life (full sample) |
|---|---|---|
| AVAX/ATOM | 119.9h | **2.56h** |
| DOT/LINK | 196.8h | **2.31h** |
| DODO/FIDA | 76.67h | **2.72h** |

### Why this matters — retroactively explains today's confusing results

Every design attempt in this entire log (Aug 2026 onward, all six today's
attempts included) has used `validated_half_life` to scale
`exit_time_stop_hours` (`2x`), `min_hold_hours`/`target_zscore`
(entry-depth-scaled off it), and today's fixed-holding-period design
(`1x half_life` = 120h/197h/77h). All of them calibrated against a number
30-80x too large for the process actually being traded. This pair of
prior observations now has an explanation, not just a description:

- The original "Why entry_ic won't move" investigation noted median hold
  time was "2-3h... vs a 120h half-life" and treated the 2-3h figure as a
  property of the series' own oscillation frequency. It is closer to the
  ACTUAL half-life (2.3-2.7h) than the 120h figure it was being compared
  against — the strategy was accidentally exiting near the real
  reversion timescale under the old TARGET-crossing design; the "vs 120h
  half-life" framing was the side that was wrong.
- Today's fixed-holding design held every position 30-80x past its actual
  expected reversion point. The bulk of every holding period (from ~15h,
  a generous multiple of the real ~2.5h half-life, out to 120-197h) was
  exposure to whatever the spread randomly did AFTER it had already
  reverted — noise, not edge capture. This is a far more direct
  explanation for the erratic, non-replicating cross-pair pattern (83rd /
  32nd / 48th percentile) than any pair-specific "relationship health"
  story: the holding period itself was wrong by nearly two orders of
  magnitude, for all three pairs simultaneously.

### Other findings from the same pass, secondary but real

- **Fat tails, not Gaussian.** Excess kurtosis 16.2 (AVAX/ATOM) / 13.3
  (DOT/LINK) / 37.8 (DODO/FIDA); Jarque-Bera rejects normality on every
  series (p≈0). The z-score's implicit "how extreme is this deviation"
  calibration assumes something closer to Gaussian than any of these
  series actually are — DODO/FIDA most severely (one spread observation
  at 0.43 against a typical 99th-percentile of 0.05).
- **Stationarity confirmed by two independent tests, not one.** ADF and
  KPSS agree the kalman_spread is stationary on all three pairs — the
  core mean-reversion premise itself is not in question.
- **DODO/FIDA's legs are much less correlated and less stably so.**
  Full-sample return correlation: AVAX/ATOM 0.71, DOT/LINK 0.78,
  DODO/FIDA only 0.34, with a far less stable rolling 30-day correlation
  (std 0.22, range -0.13 to 0.92, vs 0.09-0.11 for the other two pairs).
  DODO/FIDA passes the formal cointegration tests but rests on a
  meaningfully weaker, less stable economic relationship between its two
  legs — a plausible, independent reason its fixed-holding result landed
  at the noise floor (48th percentile) even with a "healthy"-looking stop
  rate.

### Recommendation

Before any further exit-rule design work: re-derive `validated_half_life`
from the KALMAN spread directly (the series actually traded), not the
static OLS training-window figure, and re-test the fixed-holding design
(and potentially the original entry-depth-scaled min-hold/target design)
calibrated to a ~2-3h reversion timescale instead of 77-197h. This is a
measurement correction, not a seventh parameter guess — it changes what
number every existing design attempt was already trying to use correctly.

### Implemented and re-tested — the correction resolves the confusing cross-pair pattern, into a clean, unanimous rejection

`bot/research/kalman_filter_engine.py` gains `estimate_kalman_half_life()`
— the same AR(1)/OU regression `cointegration_engine.py` already uses,
applied to `kalman_spread` instead of the static spread, computed and
saved (`{pair}_kalman_half_life.csv`) on every future Kalman run, real
tests included (planted-lambda recovery, non-reverting-series safety).
Re-ran the fixed-holding design (Sixth design attempt) with each pair's
CORRECT half-life instead of the static-OLS figure: exit_time_stop_hours
= 3h / 2h / 3h (AVAX/ATOM / DOT/LINK / DODO/FIDA) instead of 120h/197h/77h.

Self-audit (full-period, before the permutation test): all three pairs
now show much MORE consistent behavior than under the wrong half-life —
stop rates converge to 5.3% / 4.5% / 5.4% (were 21.3% / 29.7% / 21.5%),
win rates converge to 82.1% / 77.6% / 81.4% (were more scattered).

**Permutation test, all three pairs, 100 shuffles each:**

| metric | AVAX/ATOM | DOT/LINK | DODO/FIDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| win_rate %ile | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| sharpe_ratio %ile | **0.0** | **0.0** | **0.0** |
| profit_factor %ile | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| real_entry_ic | 0.132 | 0.159 | 0.154 |
| `edge_appears_real` | False | False | False |

Every one of 300 total shuffles beat the real result on every metric, on
all three pairs, uniformly — the cleanest, most cross-pair-consistent
rejection of any of the seven exit-rule variants tried in this log.
`entry_ic` remains real and significant, but smaller than under the
(wrong) 77-197h window (0.13-0.16 vs 0.25-0.44) — consistent with there
simply being less time, over a genuinely short ~2-3h hold, for the
entry-depth relationship to convert into realised return.

### Final interpretation

The confusing, inconsistent cross-pair pattern from the uncorrected
fixed-holding attempt (83rd / 32nd / 48th percentile on Sharpe) was
itself an artifact of calibrating against an arbitrary, wrong number, not
evidence of real pair-specific structure. Correcting the measurement did
not rescue the design — it resolved the ambiguity into a clean, unanimous
"no." This is the right outcome to trust: a consistent negative result
across three independent pairs, on the correctly-calibrated version of
the design, is far stronger evidence than an inconsistent maybe ever was.

**Status: this closes the fixed-holding-period line of investigation with
a clean rejection.** Seven exit-rule variants now tested (six on the
threshold-crossing family, one fixed-holding, the last one at both a
wrong and a corrected half-life). `entry_ic` (~0.13-0.44 depending on
holding window, always positive, always significant, replicated across
three independent pairs and every design tried) remains the only thing
that has held up throughout. The blocker has never been the entry signal.

## Pipeline-wide audit — zero-execution-lag assumption, real and systemic, but severity depends entirely on forecast horizon

**Date:** 2026-09-15

Following the fixed-holding rejection, tried something fundamentally
different: a continuous, always-in-market factor portfolio (weight
continuously proportional to `-kalman_zscore`, no threshold, no discrete
trades, real transaction costs on turnover) instead of another exit-rule
variant on the discrete-trade design. Full audit trail below, because the
first version of this test was itself wrong in an instructive way.

### First attempt: an invalid permutation test, not a finding

Block-shuffling `(kalman_zscore, kalman_spread)` pairs — the same
mechanism `permutation_test_engine.py` already uses — does NOT null out
this design's apparent edge: shuffled `corr(z, spread_change)` measured
-0.323, indistinguishable from (even slightly stronger than) the real
-0.303. Diagnosed why: this design bets every candle on the IMMEDIATE
1-step reversion of an already-known-stationary, strongly autocorrelated
series (ACF(1)~0.70-0.74) — that relationship is a LOCAL, moment-to-moment
property true almost everywhere in a mean-reverting series, not a
calendar-specific pattern, so any block-preserving shuffle leaves it
intact. This is a genuine methodological dead end for THIS design, not a
result in either direction — recorded here so it is not silently retried.

### Second attempt: real out-of-sample walk-forward, sidesteps the shuffle problem

Split each pair's full history into 5 consecutive, non-overlapping 1-year
windows (matching `walk_forward_engine.py`'s own `TEST_WINDOW_YEARS=1`
convention) — no parameter fit on any "train" portion (the weight rule is
fixed), each window a genuinely untouched chronological slice.

**Result: implausibly strong and implausibly consistent** — Sharpe
positive in 15/15 folds across three pairs, ranging 8.08-17.83, real
transaction costs included. Numbers this large, this consistent, on a
liquid, widely-traded pair, across bull/bear/sideways regimes, for 5
years, are exactly the signature that should trigger maximum skepticism,
not celebration — nothing this easy to execute persists uncaptured in a
reasonably efficient market for that long.

### The check that mattered: execution latency

This design assumes you observe `kalman_zscore` at a candle's close and
execute AT THAT SAME CLOSE — zero latency. Added a real execution-lag
parameter (weight decided at t, applied only `lag` candles later) and
re-ran:

| exec_lag | AVAX/ATOM Sharpe | DOT/LINK Sharpe | DODO/FIDA Sharpe |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0h (original) | 13.21 | 12.56 | 10.34 |
| 1h | 9.22 | 6.93 | 7.89 |
| 2h | 6.28 | 3.79 | 5.78 |
| 4h | 1.48 | **-0.65** | 2.78 |

Sharpe collapses toward zero (DOT/LINK goes negative) within a few hours
of realistic execution delay. The entire apparent edge was concentrated
in the unrealistic zero-latency assumption for this 1-hour-ahead design.
**Verdict: this continuous factor-portfolio direction is REJECTED** — not
by permutation (which couldn't test it validly) but by a direct,
decisive execution-realism check.

### The bigger question this forced: is the zero-lag assumption present elsewhere in the pipeline?

Checked directly. **Yes, systemically, in two places:**

1. `entry_exit_engine.py`'s `_scan_candles()`: `entry_spread = spread` uses
   THIS SAME candle's spread value as both the signal source and the fill
   price — zero latency between decision and execution. Present since
   this engine's inception; not something introduced by any design
   attempt in this log.
2. `kalman_filter_engine.py`'s `_calculate_forward_returns()`:
   `spread_forward_Nh[t] = spread[t+N] - spread[t]` — the exact same
   assumption, present in the ORIGINAL "IC=0.5043 EXCELLENT" claim that
   founded this entire research program, before any pair in this log was
   ever tested.

**Neither of these reverses any of the seven rejections above** —
realistic execution lag can only make an already-rejected discrete-trade
design look worse, never better, so the discrete-trade verdicts stand
and would likely be even more decisively rejected under a stricter,
lag-aware simulation.

**But the ORIGINAL foundational IC claim needed the same direct check,
and the result is genuinely different from the 1-hour design above:**

| exec_lag | AVAX/ATOM IC(signal, spread_forward_4h) | DOT/LINK | DODO/FIDA |

### CORRECTION (2026-09-15, same day) — the self-audit above was wrong; both the original results below and this correction's discovery process are recorded for the honest paper trail

The original "Result" and "Verdict" subsections immediately below this
note (the DOT/LINK-near-miss table) were **computed on a broken test and
are void.** Two independent bugs were found while trying to extend this
same design into a 7-pair cross-sectional portfolio test:

1. **`exit_time_stop_hours` silently defaulted to 240h, not 4h.**
   `permutation_test_engine.py`'s `run()` only calls `load_pair_config()`
   (which resolves the real half-life, and from it `exit_time_stop_hours`)
   when the kalman CSV's filename parses as
   `<SYMBOL_A>_<SYMBOL_B>_kalman.csv`. Every file built for this design was
   named `..._kalman_LAG1h.csv` (the execution-lag variant) — which never
   matches that pattern, so the lookup was silently skipped entirely and
   every engine fell back to the class default `exit_time_stop_hours=240`.
   Directly inspecting a saved trade log confirmed `hours_held` clustering
   near 240h (mean 190h), not 4h. The self-audit paragraph above, claiming
   "TIMESTOP exits cluster at exactly 4.0h," was itself wrong — it was
   never re-checked against the actual saved output. **This design was
   never actually testing a 4-hour hold.** Fixed by extracting the
   filename parser's registry-backed symbol-splitting logic into a reusable
   `_split_pair_string()` helper (`entry_exit_engine.py`) and falling back
   to parsing the `pair_name` argument itself when the filename doesn't
   follow convention.

2. **`kalman_spread` itself was being shuffled in the permutation null.**
   `_block_shuffle()` shuffled every column in `self._shuffle_columns`,
   including `kalman_spread` — the fill-price/PnL-determining series, not
   a decision input. Reassigning its values to random timestamps destroys
   the real, serially-mean-reverting price process's autocorrelation and
   tail structure — exactly what produces genuine adverse excursions and
   stop-losses. Confirmed empirically after fixing bug 1 alone: with
   `exit_time_stop_hours` correctly at 4h, 100/100 shuffles *still* beat
   the real win rate (83-89% shuffled vs. 74.8% real) and Sharpe on every
   pair — a one-directional artifact, not noise. Every shuffled "null"
   world was artificially easier to trade than reality because it had lost
   the real spread's tail risk. Fixed by excluding `kalman_spread` from the
   shuffle, keeping it at its true chronological values — the correct null
   only scrambles *when* the decision was made, never *what the market
   actually did* — matching the precedent already established in this
   session's own `make_lagged_kalman.py` convention ("the one column that
   must stay real/unshifted"). The same bug existed independently in the
   7-pair portfolio script's own hand-rolled shuffle and was fixed there
   too.

Both fixes are permanent, in `bot/research/permutation_test_engine.py`
and `bot/research/entry_exit_engine.py`, not one-off monkeypatches.

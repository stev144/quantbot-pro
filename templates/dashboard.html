How these z-scores are calculated
  • Return: crypto — Binance's rolling 24 h change, each coin's window ending at its last trade (coins with no trade in 2 h are excluded and counted above); forex — each currency's 24 h change against USD from its USD pair.
  • z = (r − mean) ÷ standard deviation, across the eligible universe at this moment; sample standard deviation (n − 1). Same definition as the research cross-section engine; at least {{ intel.min_assets }} assets are required and zero variance shows "unavailable".
  • No outlier trimming: raw returns (the research engine's winsorisation needs each coin's history). One extreme coin widens σ and shrinks everyone else's |z|.
  • Not a signal. A large |z| means unusual relative to the others today. It is not a predicted reversal, a mispricing or evidence of alpha; cross-sectional strategy research on this universe found no surviving edge after costs.
  • The research pipeline's own 1-hour cross-section (historical, before the sealed holdout) is on Market Intelligence.

How was this regime classified?

The detector looks at the latest one-hour candles and asks three questions. Is there a strong trend? (ADX of 25 or more.) Are the 20- and 50-hour averages far apart? (Gap of 0.5% of price or more.) Is the price range wide rather than squeezed? (Bollinger width above 2% of price.)

If two or three say "trend", the market is trending; whether up or down comes from the 20-hour average being above or below the 50-hour one. If two or three say "range", it is ranging. One check comes first: if the recent price range (ATR) is 1.7 times or more its own 50-hour average, the market is high volatility and no strategy is routed.

"High / medium / low" only says how many of the three agreed. It is not the chance the label is right. These are descriptions of the recent past, not forecasts.
